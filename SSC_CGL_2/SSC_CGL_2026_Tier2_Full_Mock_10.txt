Question:
The price of sugar is increased by 25%. By what percent should a household reduce consumption so that expenditure remains the same?

Options:
A) 20%
B) 25%
C) 15%
D) 30%

Correct Answer:
A) 20%

Detailed Solution:
- Let original price = P, consumption = C, expenditure = PC
- New price = 1.25P
- Let new consumption = C'
- Expenditure same: 1.25P × C' = PC
- C' = PC/(1.25P) = C/1.25 = 0.8C
- Reduction = C - 0.8C = 0.2C = 20% of C
- Shortcut: % reduction = (increase/(100+increase)) × 100
- = (25/125) × 100 = 20%

A shopkeeper sells two articles at ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. What is his overall gain or loss percentage?

Options:
A) 1% loss
B) 1% gain
C) No profit no loss
D) 2% loss

Correct Answer:
A) 1% loss

Detailed Solution:
- Article 1 (10% gain): CP₁ = 990/1.1 = ₹900
- Article 2 (10% loss): CP₂ = 990/0.9 = ₹1100
- Total CP = 900 + 1100 = ₹2000
- Total SP = 990 + 990 = ₹1980
- Loss = 2000 - 1980 = ₹20
- Loss % = (20/2000) × 100 = 1%
- Note: When SP is same and gain/loss % is same, there's always a loss
- Formula: Loss% = (common %)²/100 = 100/100 = 1%
