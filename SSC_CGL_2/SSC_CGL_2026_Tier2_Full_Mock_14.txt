Question:
From the top of a tower 100 m high, the angles of depression of two cars parked on the same side of the tower are 45° and 30°. What is the distance (in m) between the two cars? (Use √3 = 1.732)

Options:
A) 73.2 m
B) 732 m
C) 173.2 m
D) 100 m

Correct Answer:
A) 73.2 m

Detailed Solution:
Step 1: Let the height of tower h = 100m.
Step 2: Let distances of cars from base be x and y.
   For angle 45°: tan 45° = h/x => 1 = 100/x => x = 100 m.
   For angle 30°: tan 30° = h/y => 1/√3 = 100/y => y = 100√3 m.
Step 3: Distance between cars = y - x = 100√3 - 100 = 100(√3 - 1).
Step 4: Calculate value.
   100(1.732 - 1) = 100(0.732) = 73.2 m.
   Correct match.

Question:
A solid metallic sphere of radius 10.5 cm is melted and recast into a number of smaller cones, each of radius 3.5 cm and height 3 cm. Find the number of cones so formed.

Options:
A) 126
B) 144
C) 105
D) 252

Correct Answer:
A) 126

Detailed Solution:
Step 1: Volume of Sphere = (4/3) * pi * R^3
   R = 10.5 = 21/2 cm.
   Vol_S = (4/3) * pi * (21/2)^3.
Step 2: Volume of one Cone = (1/3) * pi * r^2 * h
   r = 3.5 = 7/2 cm.
   h = 3 cm.
   Vol_C = (1/3) * pi * (7/2)^2 * 3.
Step 3: Number of cones (N) = Vol_S / Vol_C.
   N = [ (4/3) * pi * (21/2)^3 ] / [ (1/3) * pi * (7/2)^2 * 3 ]
   Cancel pi and (1/3):
   N = [ 4 * (21/2)^3 ] / [ (7/2)^2 * 3 ]
   = [ 4 * (21*21*21)/8 ] / [ (49/4) * 3 ]
   = [ (21*21*21)/2 ] / [ (147/4) ]
   = [ (9261)/2 ] * [ 4/147 ]
   = 9261 * 2 / 147.
   
   Let's simplify differently.
   R = 3r (since 10.5 = 3 * 3.5).
   Vol_S = (4/3) pi (3r)^3 = (4/3) pi 27r^3 = 36 pi r^3.
   Vol_C = (1/3) pi r^2 h.
   N = (36 pi r^3) / ( (1/3) pi r^2 h ) = (36 * 3 * r) / h = 108r / h.
   r = 3.5, h = 3.
   N = 108 * 3.5 / 3 = 36 * 3.5.
   36 * 3 = 108.
   36 * 0.5 = 18.
   108 + 18 = 126.

The rise of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC) marks a significant shift in the global financial landscape. Unlike cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, which operate on decentralized networks, CBDCs are issued and regulated by a country's central bank. This gives them the status of legal tender, offering the safety and liquidity of physical cash but in a digital form. Proponents argue that CBDCs can enhance financial inclusion, reduce transaction costs, and improve the efficiency of cross-border payments. Moreover, programmable money could ensure that welfare payments are used for their intended purpose, reducing leakage and corruption.

However, the transition is not without risks. Privacy concerns top the list, as central banks could potentially track every transaction, leading to a surveillance state. Unlike cash, which offers anonymity, digital transactions leave a footprint. Furthermore, if citizens move their deposits from commercial banks to central bank wallets during a crisis, it could destabilize the banking sector, leading to a credit crunch. Cybersecurity is another critical area; a successful cyberattack on the central bank's infrastructure could cripple the entire economy. Therefore, policymakers must tread carefully, balancing innovation with stability and privacy rights.
